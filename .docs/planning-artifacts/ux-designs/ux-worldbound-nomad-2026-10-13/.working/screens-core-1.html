Screens · core set 1 · built on Direction A3

Core screens, first pass

Every frame uses the approved A3 language: warm paper, navy ink and ochre, the WORLDBOUND wordmark with the compass-rose O, the calendar Day chip, round code stamps, and the passport strip. The mode appears only on the passport cover. Each top bar has a menu button next to the logo, drawn small with a 44 px hit area.

  • Ingrid (UJ-1, Explorer): Bergen → Copenhagen → Paris → London → Rome → Istanbul → home. She's in Paris on Day 5 with €1,270, and in Istanbul on Day 15 with €673.
  • Jonas (UJ-2, Explorer): he calls home from Rome with €20 and ends with 3 stamps.
Section 1 · FR-3, FR-4

Map: the final combined layout, and the Europe zoom

This combines MAP-1's slim in-flow passport strip with MAP-2's map treatment and MAP-2's compact city-day card. When no route is open, the bottom 318 px is a resting panel. That's the same footprint as the route sheet, so the map never jumps when a sheet opens or closes.

Section 2 · FR-5, FR-10

Arriving in Paris: two ways to show the fact card

Both show Ingrid stepping off the bus from Copenhagen on Day 5 with €1,270 left. The text and actions are the same in both. Only the stage differs.

Section 3 · FR-1, FR-2

Start, intro and menu

The start screen leads to the one-screen intro on a first visit, then to the map. "How to play" on the start screen and in the menu opens the same intro.

Section 4 · FR-10, FR-22

Passport: Stamps and Album

Tapping the passport strip opens this screen. Day complete's "View album" link lands on the Album tab. The state is Day 6 with €1,170, right after Paris (CD2).

Section 5 · FR-7, FR-11

Travel home: one last check

Before the trip home is booked, the sheet shows the stamp count, whether the goal will be reached, and that the journey ends on landing (FR-7). Ingrid is in Istanbul on Day 15 with €673, and has chosen the plane.

Section 6 · FR-14, FR-11, FR-12

Journey summary

Both journeys get the same warm frame: the passport, the photos, the numbers, and a way to go again. Not reaching the goal is stated plainly and never punished.
