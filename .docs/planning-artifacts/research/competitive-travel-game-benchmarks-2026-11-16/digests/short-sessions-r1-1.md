# Digest: short sessions, persistence without accounts, replay (round 1, agent 1)

Scope: Wordle, Reigns, browser storage facts. Evidence retrieved via web search/fetch on 2026-11-16. Sources fully read: WebKit blog, MDN, Kotaku, Game Developer (Wardle), Game Developer (Reigns: Her Majesty), Inverse (Alliot). Other items come from search-result snippets only and are marked low confidence.

## Claims

1. Josh Wardle's stated design target was "just three minutes of your time a day, and that's it." — Josh Wardle reflects on the unconventional road to Wordle's success — Game Developer (Alissa McAloon) — 2022-03-25 — https://www.gamedeveloper.com/marketing/josh-wardle-reflects-on-the-the-unconventional-road-to-wordle-s-success — accessed 2026-11-16 — high — mechanic
2. Wardle said the once-a-day limit created a shared social element that became "a huge part of the game", and that this social success was not intentional on his part. — same source as 1 — high — player-sentiment
3. Wardle on the emoji grid: "This emoji grid becomes a way in which you can share that story really easily with others"; he kept links out of the grid because of its "beautiful, simple aesthetic"; sharing made the game "a club that you're either part of or not part of." — same source as 1 — high — presentation
4. Wardle did not invent the emoji grid: an early player (reported as being in New Zealand) typed results with emoji, and he built it into the game. He reportedly rejected automatic social posting as "trashy". — search-result summaries of TechCrunch interview (2022-01-12) and Newstalk ZB — https://techcrunch.com/2022/01/12/josh-wardle-interview-wordle/ ; https://newstalkzb.co.nz/on-air/heather-du-plessis-allan-drive/audio/andrew-chen-the-wordle-emoji-grid-was-created-by-a-kiwi — accessed 2026-11-16 — medium (not read in full) — release-fact
5. Wordle stats/streaks were never stored server-side by Wardle or the NYT; they live on each player's device and browser, tied to the URL used (confirmed by NYT comms executive director Jordan Cohen). — Wordle streaks broken in NYT migration — Kotaku (Ari Notis) — 2022-02-11 — https://kotaku.com/wordle-new-york-times-streaks-broken-nyt-migration-dail-1848525236 — accessed 2026-11-16 — high — technical
6. After the Feb 2022 move to nytimes.com some players' streaks reset; NYT said the next day that "stats and streaks should be carried over for the vast majority", but redirects rolled out over several days due to caching, and some users still had problems. — same source as 5 — high — release-fact
7. Migration mechanism: the old site read stats from localStorage, passed them in the redirect URL, and the new site wrote them into its own localStorage (key `nyt-wordle-statistics`). — How Wordle kept your streak alive when it migrated to the NYT's website — DEV Community (timothee) — unknown — https://dev.to/timothee/how-wordle-kept-your-streak-alive-when-it-migrated-to-the-nyts-website-170b — accessed 2026-11-16 — medium (search snippet only) — technical
8. Streak loss was newsworthy enough for mainstream coverage ("Wordle fail", "Wordle disaster"), and a later Tom's Guide writer reported a ~180-day streak resetting during travel without a missed game, suggesting an NYT account as a safeguard. — Tom's Guide (two articles) — unknown — https://www.tomsguide.com/news/wordle-disaster-some-players-lose-their-streaks-in-transfer-to-nyt ; https://www.tomsguide.com/features/i-lost-my-wordle-streak-without-losing-or-missing-a-game-heres-what-happened — accessed 2026-11-16 — low (fetch failed; headline + search snippet only) — player-sentiment
9. Safari ITP deletes "all of a website's script-writable storage after seven days of Safari use without user interaction on the site" — covering LocalStorage, IndexedDB, SessionStorage, media keys, Service Worker registrations and cache. Days count only days Safari is used. — Full Third-Party Cookie Blocking and More — WebKit blog (John Wilander) — 2020-03-24 — https://webkit.org/blog/10218/full-third-party-cookie-blocking-and-more/ — accessed 2026-11-16 — high — technical
10. Home-screen web apps are exempt from the 7-day cap; they track their own days of use, and WebKit does "not expect the first-party in such a web application to have its website data deleted." — same source as 9 — high — technical
11. MDN: in private browsing modes "stored data is usually deleted when the private browsing mode ends"; quotas may differ. — Storage quotas and eviction criteria — MDN — unknown (living doc) — https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Storage_API/Storage_quotas_and_eviction_criteria — accessed 2026-11-16 — high — technical
12. MDN: Safari evicts script-created data for an origin with no user interaction (click/tap) in the last seven days of browser use when cross-site tracking prevention is on; server-set cookies are exempt. — same source as 11 — high — technical
13. MDN: storage is "best-effort" by default (can be evicted under storage pressure, LRU); `navigator.storage.persist()` can request persistent storage — Firefox prompts, Safari/Chromium auto-decide from interaction history without prompting. localStorage is ~5 MiB per origin; exceeding throws `QuotaExceededError` (wrap in try/catch). — same source as 11 — high — technical
14. Reigns' Alliot: "You can make a hundred decisions in five minutes" — fast decision pace is what makes the design work. — Reigns developer interview (François Alliot) — Inverse (Steven T. Wright) — 2016-11-02 — https://www.inverse.com/article/23117-reigns-francois-alliot-nerial-developer-interview — accessed 2026-11-16 — high — mechanic
15. Reigns run structure: goal is "the longest reign possible"; kings are linked in a dynasty so "some elements you trigger in one reign will have impacts over the rulers"; Alliot "didn't want the player to restart the same thing and feel punished." — same source as 14 — high — mechanic
16. Reigns lets the player set the pace: "you can swipe very quickly or you can take your time"; "You're trapped in this loop, this infinite way of swiping." — Why being trapped in a loop makes Reigns: Her Majesty so satisfying — Game Developer (John Harris) — 2018-02-06 — https://gamedeveloper.com/design/why-being-trapped-in-a-loop-makes-i-reigns-her-majesty-i-so-satisfying — accessed 2026-11-16 — high — mechanic
17. A review describes Reigns as designed for short bursts; repetition is fine in short sessions but makes long sessions less appealing. — Reigns review — IBTimes UK — unknown — https://www.ibtimes.co.uk/reigns-review-devolver-digitals-mobile-king-sim-fun-shame-free-medieval-tinder-1579706 — accessed 2026-11-16 — low (search snippet only) — player-sentiment
