# 80 Days (inkle, 2014): research digest r1-1

Accessed 2026-11-16. Sources: 7 pages read in full (WebFetch) plus search-result snippets. Three pages returned 403: Giant Bomb wiki, Slant Magazine and GameSpot. PC Gamer returned no body text. Claims taken only from search-engine summaries are marked low confidence.

## Claims

1. 80 Days started as a question about branching stories: how do you convince the player that their choices are having an impact? The travel frame meant "every choice was loaded with ready-made context", because time, money and luggage are obvious travel factors. — Road to the IGF: Inkle's 80 Days (Alex Wawro, quoting Jon Ingold) — Game Developer — 2015-02-13 — https://gamedeveloper.com/design/road-to-the-igf-inkle-s-i-80-days-i- — accessed 2026-11-16 — high — mechanic
2. Ingold said the earlier map-based Sorcery! gave players choices like "Head for the river or head for the mine - who knows?", where people "didn't always have a reason to make their decision". In 80 Days, journeys are unlocked by exploring cities, which he compared to "just what backpacking is like!" — same source as 1 — high — mechanic
3. In the first design, nearly every route was visible on the globe. The team locked all routes and added an Explore mechanic, so city content became optional and routes became rewards, for example a camel bought from a merchant. The postmortem credits this change with fixing the sense that the game wasn't fun and with making it feel player-driven. — Postmortem: Inkle's 80 Days (Jon Ingold and Joseph Humfrey) — Game Developer — 2015-10-14 — https://gamedeveloper.com/business/postmortem-inkle-s-i-80-days-i- — accessed 2026-11-16 — high — mechanic
4. Between cities the game tracks only a few things: money, health, time and three character traits. This made cities self-contained episodes that writers could produce out of order and in parallel. — same source as 3 — high — mechanic
5. The postmortem lists these problems: too much design scope, which left a "far more tangled and complex" codebase; content growth that turned Europe into "a fearsome spaghetti-maze"; a conversation system prototyped late and nearly cut; market and pricing systems left unpolished because of limited time and money; and only "seven or eight" testers, too few to catch story-breaking bugs. — same source as 3 — high — mechanic
6. Save files store the full history of the player's choices, not just the current state. An in-game feedback button attached the save file automatically and was kept after beta to diagnose story bugs. — same source as 3 — high — mechanic
7. The postmortem says the visual style draws on Art Deco travel posters instead of conventional steampunk, partly to match iOS 7's flat design. Ingold called the style "Victorian Futurism" in another interview, which I saw only as a search snippet. — same source as 3 (search snippet: Grab It Magazine interview, https://grabitmagazine.com/blog/post/interview-with-gamebook-developer-inkle-on-80-days/) — medium — presentation
8. Lead writer Meg Jayanth researched the cities and journeys and aimed to be "inclusive and even-handed" towards the cultures shown. — Postmortem: Inkle's 80 Days — Game Developer — 2015-10-14 — https://gamedeveloper.com/business/postmortem-inkle-s-i-80-days-i- — accessed 2026-11-16 — high — presentation
9. The game starts on Tuesday 1 October 1872. inkle described it as "part interactive fiction, part globe-based board-game", with "nearly two hundred" cities linked by many kinds of transport, including steam-boats, balloons, trains, camel-trains and elephants. Each day of travel is part of the story and can bring good or bad consequences, and every choice is "remembered for the rest of the game." — So what is 80 Days? — inkle blog — 2014-04 (exact day unknown) — https://www.inklestudios.com/2014/04/15/so-what-is-eighty-days.html — accessed 2026-11-16 — high — mechanic
10. The game does not end at day 80. Running out of money "is easy to do and can cause major delays". Money and health must be replenished regularly. Items can make travel more comfortable, reveal new routes, or "sell for fantastic profits in distant cities". Backtracking is often impossible. — 80 Days (Meg Jayanth/inkle) (Emily Short) — Emily Short's Interactive Storytelling (emshort.blog) — 2014-08-05, updated 2015-05-22 — https://emshort.blog/2014/08/05/80-days-meg-jayanth-inkle/ — accessed 2026-11-16 — high — mechanic
11. Story events often override the player's plans, such as a cholera outbreak or a city under siege. Some are optional and some are "very much forced on you". Some locations cost so much to reach that they are hard to justify visiting. — same source as 10 — high — mechanic
12. Emily Short describes the writing as "fragmentary, episodic, and textural", with few recurring characters, and says the format favours "novel settings and striking imagery" over character development. She praises it for centring "the women, servants, colonized forces, ex-slaves and non-Western characters" who were marginalised in Verne's novel. Its steampunk alternate history gives power to groups that did not have it historically, such as Zulu and Haitian technology. — same source as 10 — high — presentation
13. Money comes from banks, where withdrawing a decent sum takes a few days, and from city markets where goods are bought and sold. Hotels cost money, and sleeping rough is a workable option when funds run low. Route choice means weighing transport cost, luggage, health and onward destinations. — 80 Days Review (Harry Slater) — Pocket Gamer — 2014-07-31 — https://www.pocketgamer.com/80-days/review/ — accessed 2026-11-16 — high — mechanic
14. Pocket Gamer scored it 4.5/5 and said "The story is so good you'll want to play through it multiple times." It criticised that "sometimes the decisions you make won't have the outcomes you expect." The game also has a meta layer that shows other players' progress and routes. — same source as 13 — high — player-sentiment
15. Steam store copy: "80 DAYS is a breakneck race, with an in-game clock that never stops running"; "Choose your own route around a 3D globe"; "170 cities"; "Trade items for profit, and collect the equipment for the conditions you'll face"; "Live multiplayer feed, showing journeys of other players as they happen". The PC release was 28 Sep 2015, the developers were inkle Ltd and Cape Guy Ltd, and the page lists Steam Cloud. User reviews were "Very Positive" (91% of 1,182) when accessed. — 80 Days on Steam — Valve / inkle — 2015-09-28 (release) — https://store.steampowered.com/app/381780/ — accessed 2026-11-16 — high — release-fact
16. An IGN quote on the Steam page refers to "my three-hour adventure", which suggests one playthrough takes about three hours. — same source as 15 (IGN quote, secondhand) — medium — player-sentiment
17. The iOS version was released on 31 July 2014. It was later ported to Android and Steam, and the staggered launches hurt the game's visibility. The original was written in native Objective-C, which made a simultaneous multi-platform launch impractical. — Postmortem: Inkle's 80 Days — Game Developer — 2015-10-14 — https://gamedeveloper.com/business/postmortem-inkle-s-i-80-days-i- — accessed 2026-11-16 (iOS date from inkle press kit search snippet) — medium — release-fact
18. Players often run out of money or time because the most direct routes tend to be the most expensive. Common player advice is to make small detours to earn money and to trade more. One player got stuck in a South American town with no bank and only expensive routes out. Another reached New York with time to spare but no money and nothing to sell. — Steam Community, Quarter to Three and intfiction.org threads (search snippets only, not opened) — various — unknown — https://steamcommunity.com/app/381780/discussions/0/1621724904371155519 ; https://forum.quartertothree.com/t/80-days-thread/76165 ; https://intfiction.org/t/around-the-world-in-80-days/67228 — accessed 2026-11-16 — low — player-sentiment
19. Time passes in real time inside cities. One player said that lingering or hesitating over a decision can mean watching an already-paid departure leave without you. — intfiction.org thread (search snippet) — intfiction.org — unknown — https://intfiction.org/t/around-the-world-in-80-days/67228 — accessed 2026-11-16 — low — mechanic. The Steam copy in claim 15 ("clock that never stops running") partly supports this.
20. Slant Magazine argued that the game's playful treatment of history makes it digestible, but that "it has its limits in terms of cultural understanding and respect." — Review: 80 Days (Jed Pressgrove) — Slant Magazine — unknown — https://www.slantmagazine.com/games/80-days/ — accessed 2026-11-16 (search snippet only; the page returned 403) — low — presentation
21. The Mental Attic named the inventory system and slow text as weaknesses. Another review snippet said the reviewer did not feel they had much control over what happened. — The Mental Attic review (2015-10-15) and a review in the Pocket Gamer search results (search snippets) — various — https://thementalattic.com/2015/10/15/review-80-days — accessed 2026-11-16 — low — player-sentiment
